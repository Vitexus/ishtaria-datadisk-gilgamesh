# The temple of Gareus (Uruk), Parthian period

Source: K. Jakubiak, *The Gareus Temple from Uruk: Some Observations Concerning the Temple Attribution*, Swiatowit LXII (2024),
109-118 (`TheGareustemplefromUruk...pdf`). The figures are by Downey 1988, Crusemann et al. 2013 and others and are only a reference
here; nothing is copied from them.

## What the article says (checked against the text)

- **Date**: a Greek inscription inside names the god Gareus and is dated 422 of the Seleucid era = 110/111 AD (some read it as
  175/176 AD). The temple is attributed to him only by that inscription. The god may be the old fire god Girru (Akkadian *girru*,
  fire), who was believed to live in the Irigal; the worshippers, the Dollamenoi, may have come from northern Mesopotamia.
- **Place**: on a hill south-west of the main hill of Uruk, south-east of the two great temple areas Bit Res and Irigal, separated
  from the main tell by a hollow. It stood in a residential quarter that was already built (houses found round it, ground raised
  to the north and west). No earlier structures are known under it.
- **Temenos**: burnt brick, roughly square, about 60 x 52 m, built like a fortification (not like any known temenos): semicircular
  half-towers, two on each side, round corner towers, buttresses; narrow rooms of similar size along the whole inner side of the
  wall. The entrance is in the east face, on the axis of the temple; the gate itself has not survived.
- **Temple**: small, 10.5 x 13.7 m, in the middle of the court, with pilasters or half-columns, corner elements, a niche at the back
  of the main axis with the remains of a podium for the statue, and six free-standing columns in front of the facade (decorative,
  carrying nothing; aligned so that from the entrance the second column from the left and the right line up with the corners: the
  temple looks bigger). Two reconstructions of its facade by Downey (an arched central opening between columns; a stepped
  parapet over a band of ornament). A cornice brick shows a winged lion-scorpion-dragon creature (a Mesopotamian composite).
- **Surroundings**: south of the temenos a trapezoidal fortified part with buttresses, bastions and square corner towers and, behind
  it, a large rectangular building round a courtyard with narrow rooms and a small dwelling squeezed between the walls; in front
  of the gate a rectangular gate building with buttresses and an empty courtyard.
- Parallels named: the Assur peripteros temple, the Barmaran and Shahiru temples of Hatra.

## For this datadisk

The temple is about 2800 years later than Gilgamesh (c. 2700 BCE). It is not in the datadisk. Adding it would be a deliberate fantasy
anachronism like the Babylonian Ishtar Gate and Etemenanki (owner decision) and would have to be marked so in the story text.
