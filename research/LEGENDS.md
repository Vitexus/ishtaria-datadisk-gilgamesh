# The other legends of the disk

Besides the Atra-Hasis epic, Enuma Elish and Ishtar's Descent (research/ATRAHASIS.md, ENUMA_ELISH.md, ISHTAR_DESCENT.md),
five legends lie as clay tablets in the world (`places/legend_tablets.yaml`, 21 tablets; `models-src/legend_tablets/`).
Reading all tablets of a legend earns its achievement (`read_all`); reading all 37 tablets of the eight legends earns
`keeper_of_legends`. The elders of Uruk and of the hunter's village tell them (`dialogues/elder_tales_man.yaml`,
`elder_tales_woman.yaml`). The texts are our own retelling and quote nothing.

| Legend | Achievement | Tablets | Places |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Epic of Gilgamesh | `twelve_tablets` | 12 (SB I-XII) | hunter waterhole, Uruk market, palace (twice), Cedar Forest, Humbaba's lair, Ishtar's temple, Eanna, Scorpion Gate, Waters of Death, Utnapishtim's dwelling, Uruk pasture |
| Etana | `etana_tablets` | 3 | Kish (three benches) |
| Adapa | `adapa_tablets` | 2 | Eridu, the Anu ziggurat of Uruk |
| Enki and Ninhursag | `dilmun_tablets` | 2 | Eridu, Adab |
| Nergal and Ereshkigal | `nergal_tablets` | 2 | Nippur, Larsa |

## Sources

* **Gilgamesh.** The eBL editions of the Standard Babylonian epic by A. R. George (`research/epic/ebl/SB_I.md` to `SB_XII.md`;
  copyrighted, cited, not copied) and the quests of the disk. The disk plays the epic as the present of its characters, so
  the tablets (written down centuries after the king) are a deliberate overlay, like the other tablets of the world.
* **Etana.** Wikipedia (Etana): the King List says of him that he ascended to heaven; Old Babylonian copies from Susa and Tell
  Harmal, Middle Assyrian from Assur, the standard version from Nineveh. A seal of the Akkadian period shows the myth.
* **Adapa.** Wikipedia (Adapa): the oldest tradition (Tell Haddad, Sumerian), fragments from Amarna, Ashurbanipal's library.
  The ending is lost. (Wikipedia says Anu offers "bread and water of life"; the text has Ea warning of food of death.)
* **Enki and Ninhursag.** Wikipedia (Enki and Ninhursag): two copies, from Nippur and Ur. Ninhursag is the goddess of Adab.
* **Nergal and Ereshkigal.** Spoken Past (C. Pannell), "Nergal and Ereshkigal", on EA 357 (Amarna, 14th century BCE) and the
  Sultantepe Assyrian version (Gurney 1960); the page warns against mixing the two, and the tablet's card keeps them apart.
  Nergal had cults at Nippur, Uruk, Larsa and Ur; his chief temple, E-meslam, stood in Kutha (not a place of the disk).

## Notes

* None of these legends has numbered tablets in the sources except the Gilgamesh epic (twelve); the numbers of the others are
  the disk's own cutting into episodes. The first line of each face carries a key sign (LUGAL, AN, A, KI, KUR) and the second
  as many strokes as the tablet's number; textures are regenerated by `make_textures.py` and not committed.
* The tablets stand 10 to 45 m from their place (`tolerance_m: 0`, "surveyed points"); not checked against the buildings
  of the cities, or on the far places (Cedar Forest, Mount Mashu) against the world's relief.
* Cross-checks with the existing cards: Eridu holds one tablet of each of Atra-Hasis, Enuma Elish, Ishtar's Descent,
  Adapa and Enki and Ninhursag, at different bearings (110, 250, 300, 340, 60 degrees).

## The author's disclaimer

`places/author_disclaimer.yaml`: one more tablet at Eridu (bearing 150 degrees, 22 m), in the voice of a humble servant of
Enki who disclaims all the troubles of Ishtar (Inanna) and of her father Anu (card `author_disclaimer`, also a lore entry).
A joke of the disk, not a legend, no achievement. The father of Inanna differs by tradition: Sin in the Descent, Anu in
tablet VI of the Gilgamesh epic, which the text alludes to.

It is an easter egg: the last register of the face carries the author's signature in cuneiform, PI TE ISH (wi-te-is), "Vitex" as
near as the syllabary comes (`signature` in `models-src/legend_tablets/legends.py`). Nothing in the card points at it.
