# The Ishtar Gate of Babylon (research notes)

Model: see `models-src/architecture/ishtar_gate.py` and `media/models/architecture/ishtar_gate*.glb`.

The gate is not from Uruk or from Gilgamesh's time: it was built by Nebuchadnezzar II in Babylon (c. 575 BCE, a
lead-dated archaeomagnetic study says c. 569 BCE), two thousand years after the epic's setting. It is in the datadisk
because Ishtar is a main character and the gate is her best known monument.

## What the sources say

- A **double gate**: a smaller outer (north) gate, then a courtyard, then a larger and higher inner (south) gate.
  Only the smaller one is rebuilt in the Pergamon Museum (Berlin, finished 1930), at a reduced size.
- Outer gate **28 m wide including its two flanking towers and about 11 m deep**; inner gate 28 m wide and over 30 m deep;
  the gate rooms with the courtyard are **48 m** long in all; the room of the inner gate is 14.9 x 8.05 m; the passage is
  **4.5 m wide**, doors over 9 m high; the flanking brick walls **7 m thick**. (Koldewey's excavation, via the German
  encyclopedia entries; the sources vary a little.)
- Both gates have **two projecting flanking towers** on the side facing away from the city; Koldewey called it a brick
  structure with an **arched entrance** between two huge towers.
- Height: 15 m for the original gate according to Wikipedia, about 12 m (40 ft) with towers of about 30 m (100 ft) according to
  another source, the inner gate about 25 m; the Berlin reconstruction is **14 m high and 30 m wide**. The street was raised
  several times (to about 7 m, 10.85 m and finally 15 m above the first level), so the visible height changed; 14 m of
  foundation were found.
- **Facade:** fired brick, glazed **deep blue** (cobalt, said to imitate lapis lazuli); **alternating rows of aurochs (bulls,
  Adad) and mushussu dragons (Marduk)** in ochre-yellow, brown and white glaze; in Berlin five rows on each panel,
  top to bottom aurochs, dragon, aurochs, dragon, aurochs; an estimate for the original is 13 rows and 575 animals.
  Frames and borders in ochre-orange, turquoise, white and black, with rows of **rosettes**. Joints 1-6 mm sealed with
  bitumen. Each animal is made of dozens of moulded bricks (a lion: 46 bricks in 11 rows).
- **Processional Way:** over 800 m long, up to about 20 m wide, paved with large red and yellow stone slabs in bitumen; its
  walls (over 15 m high, according to one source) carried about **120 striding lions** (Ishtar), white with a yellow mane on
  blue, an orange border below, with yellow and black enamelled bricks and flowers; the lions on the east side step
  with the left foot, on the west side with the right.
- **Doors:** cedar leaves clad in bronze at all openings (four leaves closed the passage), roof of cedar beams; bronze
  bulls and snake figures were set at the threshold (inscription of Nebuchadnezzar II).
- The dedication inscription in blue and white glazed bricks is not modelled.

## What was built

`ishtar_gate.glb`: both gates (28 m wide, 11 m and 29 m deep, 15 m and 25 m high, 4.5 m passage with a round arch, cedar doors
open against the passage walls), the courtyard, 7 m thick curtain walls with merlons; `processional_way.glb`: 80 m of street,
20 m wide, between two walls with rows of lions walking towards the gate. Rows on the outer gate, top to bottom: aurochs,
dragon, aurochs, dragon, aurochs; the animals walk towards the passage.

## What the model assumes

Not found in the sources I could read, so chosen by the model builder after the Berlin photographs: the shape of the arch (a
round arch on the passage), the stepped crenellations on the towers and walls, the exact proportions of towers and
curtain walls, the number of rows on the inner gate, which animals face which way. The inner gate is built 25 m high.
